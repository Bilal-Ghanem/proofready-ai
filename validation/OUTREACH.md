# ProofReady AI outreach validation plan

**Sender identity:** ProofReady AI Research Team
**Purpose:** Validate whether small European service firms need a lightweight way to document role-appropriate AI literacy. This is research outreach, not a compliance audit or a claim that the recipient is deficient.

## Three concise personalised templates

### 1. AI governance / AI consultancy

**Subject:** Quick research question on AI-literacy evidence at {{company}}

Hi {{company}} team — I’m with the **ProofReady AI Research Team**. I noticed {{personalization_hook}} We’re researching how specialist AI firms keep simple, auditable evidence that people in different roles understand the AI systems they sell, assess or support. Is that already easy for you, or still mostly spread across training tools and documents? A one-line reply is useful; no sales call needed.

— ProofReady AI Research Team

### 2. DPO / compliance consultancy

**Subject:** How are advisory teams evidencing AI literacy?

Hi {{company}} team — I’m with the **ProofReady AI Research Team**. Your work in {{service_reference}} stood out. We’re testing a lightweight record for role-based AI-literacy evidence, designed to complement—not replace—legal advice or training. Would this solve a real client or internal-team problem for you, or is existing evidence already sufficient? Brief replies are welcome.

— ProofReady AI Research Team

### 3. Digital / HR consultancy

**Subject:** A brief question about AI use across client teams

Hi {{company}} team — I’m with the **ProofReady AI Research Team**. Given {{personalization_hook}}, your team may use AI differently across creative, strategy, delivery and operations. We’re researching whether agencies need a simple way to show who received which AI-use guidance, when, and for what role. Is that worth solving this year? “Yes,” “not now,” or “already covered” is enough.

— ProofReady AI Research Team

## Ten-message validation test

1. **Use only ten high-priority rows:** Deeper Insights, aiEthix, Eticas, The DPO Centre, Privacy Culture, PrivacySolved, Pembroke Privacy, Trilateral Research, Considerati and Found.
2. **Send in two balanced batches of five** on different weekday mornings in each recipient’s local time. Batch A: three compliance/AI firms and two others; Batch B mirrors the mix. This reduces timing and segment bias.
3. **Use the most relevant template**, replacing every variable with the CSV hook or a short fact verified on the company’s own site. Keep body under 110 words.
4. **One destination per company:** use only the listed general business email or contact form. Address the team, not an inferred individual.
5. **No links or attachments in the first message.** Ask one research question and make a one-line reply sufficient.
6. **Record before sending:** company, segment, template, date/time, destination, and the exact personalisation fact used.
7. **Follow up once only** after five business days if there is no reply: “Just closing the loop on the research question below. No reply needed if it is not relevant; we won’t follow up again.”
8. **Classify replies:** positive/problem confirmed, neutral/already solved, not now, negative/opt-out, or out-of-office. Preserve only business-relevant notes.
9. **Stop immediately** for any opt-out, complaint, invalid destination or request not to be contacted; suppress that destination from future tests.
10. **Review after all ten initial messages and eligible follow-ups** against the thresholds below before expanding the list.

## Ethical guardrails

- Be truthful about identity, research stage and purpose; never imply endorsement, prior contact, regulatory authority, customer status or a discovered compliance failure.
- Contact only published business channels. Do not scrape, enrich or infer personal emails, names, phone numbers or social profiles.
- Use the public facts in `PROSPECTS.csv` only after a quick same-day check; sites and service descriptions can change.
- Personalise around publicly stated services, never sensitive traits or surveillance-style observations.
- Keep the ask optional and low pressure. Include the sender identity and a working reply address; honour opt-outs immediately.
- One initial message and at most one follow-up. No automated sequences, tracking pixels, hidden redirects or attachment beacons.
- Do not claim legal compliance, certification, guaranteed EU AI Act readiness or replacement of legal advice.
- Minimise retention: keep company, public destination, delivery/reply category and short product-learning notes; delete message content when no longer needed.
- Before sending, confirm the outreach basis and e-privacy/direct-marketing requirements for the sender and recipient jurisdictions. This plan is not legal advice.

## Tracking metrics

Track counts and rates for:

- attempted, delivered and bounced;
- replies excluding automatic responses;
- **positive/problem-confirming replies**;
- neutral/already-solved, not-now and negative/opt-out replies;
- positive-reply rate = positive replies / delivered;
- total human-reply rate = human replies / delivered;
- bounce rate and complaint/opt-out count;
- result by segment and template;
- recurring language about current tools, evidence gaps, buyer requests, urgency and willingness to review a prototype.

With only ten messages, treat percentages as directional—not statistically conclusive.

## Stop / iterate / go thresholds

- **STOP:** any spam complaint or privacy concern; two or more explicit negative/opt-out replies; or bounce rate above 20%. Fix targeting, channel and copy before any further contact.
- **ITERATE:** zero or one positive reply, but at least two neutral/not-now human replies that identify a concrete objection or existing solution. Revise the proposition, then run a fresh, non-overlapping ten-company test.
- **GO to a second 10-message test:** at least two positive/problem-confirming replies from ten delivered, **or** at least three human replies including one positive plus a specific request to see details or a prototype; no complaints and no more than one bounce.
- Do not interpret silence as consent or product validation. Expansion means another small test, not a bulk campaign.

## Zero-cost reply path without exposing a personal phone

Use a dedicated free email inbox branded **ProofReady AI Research Team** (for example, a no-cost Proton Mail mailbox) solely for this validation. Put that email in the signature and use it as the reply-to address; do not publish or forward replies to WhatsApp, and do not include a personal mobile number. Enable multi-factor authentication with an authenticator app or security key rather than presenting a phone number publicly. If a new mailbox cannot be created without sharing information contrary to the provider’s terms or the user’s privacy preference, pause outreach until a suitable team inbox exists.

No messages should be sent until the sender inbox, jurisdictional review and suppression log are ready.
